Answers "make the mails not go to spam" with the honest version of that: no
setting guarantees the inbox. Placement is a receiver judgement about domain
reputation. What is enforceable is removing every known reason to be filtered,
and refusing to send when the signals that predict filtering go bad.

dns_auth.py replaces the pre-0.2 spf_ok/dkim_ok/dmarc_ok booleans, which a human
ticked. A checkbox is not verification; the operator ticking it is usually the
one who misconfigured the record. This resolves the actual TXT records and
checks:
  - SPF: present, exactly one (two records is a permerror, worse than none),
    no +all, within the RFC 7208 ten-lookup limit
  - DKIM: key present, p= not empty (an empty p= REVOKES the key), 2048-bit,
    not stuck in t=y test mode
  - DMARC: present (Gmail and Yahoo require it from bulk senders), valid policy
  - ALIGNMENT, which most guides omit and which is the usual cause of
    "everything looks configured but mail still lands in spam": SPF
    authenticates the envelope sender, not the visible From:. A subdomain setup
    aligns in relaxed mode and FAILS in strict.

deliverability.py enforces what decides placement once auth passes:
  - RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe. Without List-Unsubscribe-Post, Gmail shows
    no unsubscribe button and the recipient clicks "report spam" instead --
    the fastest way to destroy a sending domain.
  - construction: plain-text alternative required, image-only blocked, link
    density, URL shorteners, shouting subjects, fake Re: subjects, postal
    address present in the body
  - reputation: complaint rate pauses at 0.1%, a third of Gmail's published
    0.3% ceiling, because by the time you measure 0.3% you are already being
    filtered. Hard bounces pause at 2%. Rates are ignored below 50 sends, or
    one complaint in ten would pause a healthy campaign.
  - warm-up: a 20-day volume ramp. A new domain at full volume looks exactly
    like a compromised one.

Wired into the outbox worker at the send boundary, not offered as advice.
Reputation and warm-up blocks DEFER; construction and auth failures BLOCK.
