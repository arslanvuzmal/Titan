03 Point your DMARC reports at a mailbox that exists Namecheap 5 min

Your DMARC record asks for reports at admin@arslanvuzmallone.com — and you have no admin@ mailbox. Those reports have been bouncing, so you have had no visibility into who is authenticating as your domain or whether your own mail passes.

Do this after step 2, so arslan@ exists to receive them. In Namecheap: Domain List → Manage → Advanced DNS, find the existing TXT record with host _dmarc, and replace its value.

Replace this — host _dmarc, type TXTv=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:admin@arslanvuzmallone.com; fo=1
With thisv=DMARC1; p=quarantine; pct=25; rua=mailto:arslan@arslanvuzmallone.com; fo=1

That one line does two jobs: it fixes the dead reporting address, and it moves you off p=none.

04 Understand what you just changed read only

p=none tells Gmail and Microsoft to do nothing when a message fails authentication. Anyone can send mail as your domain and receivers are instructed to accept it. It monitors; it protects nothing.

p=quarantine tells them to put failures in spam. pct=25 applies it to a quarter of failing mail to start — a deliberate ramp, so if something of yours is misconfigured you find out from reports rather than from silence.

Why this is safe for you specifically

Your SPF, DKIM and alignment all pass today — I checked against live DNS, and the DKIM selector spacemail genuinely resolves rather than quietly finding nothing. Your legitimate mail already authenticates, so tightening the policy does not touch it.

In about a week, once reports show clean, raise pct=25 to pct=100. Later still, p=reject.
